Four things a photo cannot tell us

Two of these hard-gate a category. Buying the wrong side of either one means a return trip or an open wall.

  1. Shower valve brandgates a category

    Trim kits are brand-locked to the rough-in valve. A Delta trim will not go onto a Moen valve without opening the wall.

    How: Pull the existing escutcheon or check the plaster guard. Look for a stamped brand on the valve body.

  2. Vanity top hole spreadgates a category

    Both tops show three holes. 4-inch centerset and 8-inch widespread are different products and they are not interchangeable.

    How: Measure center to center on the two outer holes.

  3. Counts: interior doors, fan locations, switch devices

    Every one of these is a per-unit multiply. The budget totals mean nothing without them.

    How: Walk the house once with a tally. Doors, fan boxes, and every switch and outlet plate.

  4. Switch scope

    Going flat means swapping the devices themselves, not only the plates. Mixed switch styles in one house read unfinished.

    How: Confirm whether existing devices are being replaced or only re-plated.

The research came back 2 of 16 here. Pfister and Kohler were never researched, only one of four Moen archetypes was reached, and the single Delta trim that did get verified is the wrong product for this bathroom.

The correctionThe verified pick was Delta Ashlyn T14464, which is the TUB and shower trim and includes a tub spout. The primary is a walk-in shower with a pebble floor and no tub. Shower-only is T14264. Buying the T14464 puts a tub spout on a wall with no tub.

The deeper problem only exists on one side of the zone vote. At $120 to $180, credible brand trim in champagne bronze does not exist, Delta Ashlyn in a warm finish runs $240 to $260, and it only fits at all if the valve in the wall happens to be a Delta. In satin nickel, trim is available from every brand at every price and the showerhead already in there stays. This category is the single strongest practical argument for putting the two bathrooms on nickel.

Which metal reads most expensive, and which ages out first

Champagne bronze reads the most current in 2026 and is also named as peaking, with the fade projected 2027 to 2029. Aged brass carries the same risk harder. Matte black already plateaued and is now safe rather than premium. Satin nickel is the one classified as timeless.

So Champagne bronze wins the showing. Satin nickel wins 2029. Aged brass is the highest ceiling and the shortest half-life.

Is mixed metal read as intentional or unfinished

Intentional, and the consensus flipped since 2023. Matching every metal now reads staged. The rule is one dominant finish at 70 to 80 percent, one accent, each repeated at least twice, and grouped by function rather than by proximity.

So Warm on lighting and plumbing with satin nickel on door hardware is a safe, current mix. What reads unfinished is a warm faucet next to cool shower trim in the same bathroom. Keep every wet-wall fixture on one finish.

Sputnik in a 1967 house

Dated risk, not period charm. The fixture became visual shorthand for 2015 to 2022 flip renovations, and a mass-market reproduction does not read as authentic mid-century to anyone who knows the real thing.

So Leave it on the ballot but do not build the campaign around it. Tiered drum and wood bead both age better with this buyer pool.

Where underspending shows

Front door handleset, vanity faucets, and shower trim are the three a buyer physically touches. Underspend there and it registers in seconds. Interior knobs, switches, and fans above $170 are invisible.

So The bands are right. Spend the top of them on the three touch points and the floor on everything else.
