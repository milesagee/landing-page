The Open Loop

How a hook actually holds.

This is the engine under every piece we make. Learn it once and you will see it in every cut. It is the difference between a video people finish and a video people scroll.

An open loop raises a question the viewer needs answered. A closed statement hands them a conclusion they can take and leave. Open buys the watch. Closed loses it.

The test, run it on every hookOpen vs closed

After the first line, does the viewer have a complete takeaway, or an unanswered question? Complete takeaway means it is closed. Rewrite it until they have to stay for the answer.

Closed · they scroll
Open · they stay
This wallet sucks.
You won't believe what this wallet did for me.
Richmond has a good food scene.
You can't name three cities our size with a better food scene.
The James River is nice.
The most wrong thing people believe about Richmond is this river.
The shape you will see in every loop mapThe four beats
  1. Hook loop · 0 to 2s
    Open the core question. Topic still crystal clear in the first second. Withhold the payoff. If the hook is closed, nothing after it matters.
  2. Retention loop · ~15 to 20s
    Open a second question before the first one closes. This is what buys the middle of the video and stops the sag.
  3. Payoff
    Close the loops in the order you opened them. Land each one on a concrete number, name, or detail. An unpaid loop reads as bait and kills trust.
  4. Close
    Guide in bio, plus one engagement question pointed at the comments. The question is its own tiny loop.
The words are the script. The loop is the cut.Where you own it
  • The first frame is the hook loop. Pick the frame that opens the question, not the prettiest one. You have 1.5 seconds.
  • Caption timing opens the question. The burned-in line that raises Q1 goes up first, beat-timed. Never let it land after the answer.
  • Hold the reveal. Do not cut to the payoff early. The number or the named spot lands when the voiceover says it, not before.
  • Sequence protects the middle. The retention beat has to land before the hook pays off. Resolve the first question too fast and the middle sags.
  • Never drop a loop you opened. If the footage teases something, the cut has to deliver it. That is the trust that keeps them on the next video.
One name, a few aliases. We call it the open loop. In the Content Deck you will see the same thing labeled Loop map, and you will hear retention loop or open-loop practice. Same mechanic every time. Do not let the different names confuse you.

Content Is Science
A video is a stack of interchangeable chapters (hook, story, stat, steps). Judge the chapter, not the whole video. Keep winners, cut losers, rebuild one strong piece from proven parts.
  • Name each chapter, then read comments to see which one hit.
  • Kill outright flops. Find the commonality across your winners.
  • Collapse three okay videos into one great one from the working parts.

The View Ladder
Each view tier is unlocked by a different skill, not more effort. The wall most creators die on is bad ideation, not bad hooks.
  • Stuck under 2K: fix the idea first. It is the number one skill.
  • 3K to 10K: find the exact subjects your audience wants from you.

How to climb the view ladder
The Negative Hook
Open with a bold, slightly negative statement that challenges the viewer, from a place of love. A confident pattern interrupt makes ears perk up where a pleasant opener gets scrolled.
  • Swap "the easy way to X" for "you're not getting X because you skip this."
  • Lead with tension, not insults. No cursing needed.
  • Challenge a belief the viewer holds, then resolve it.
The negative hook
Three Hook Elements
A hook underperforms if it lacks tension, specificity, or pattern disruption. Tension earns the lean-in, specificity makes them feel seen, disruption makes it look different from the last 25 in the feed.
  • Be concrete in the first line. A named place signals "this is for you."
  • Design a visual break: bold opening line, on-screen text, slow zoom.
  • Audit every hook against all three before you cut.
A great hook is non-negotiable
Cult Brand: The Common Enemy
Every cult following forms around a common enemy. Tell people what to stand against, not just what you stand for. That polarity is the movement.
  • The enemy here is mediocrity: agents who do not know Richmond.
  • Say the thing most believe but are afraid to voice.
  • Accept being disliked by some to be loved by the right ones.
Dark psychology for a cult following
Five Levels of Counter-Positioning
Differentiation is survival. Counter-position across five independent levers, and stack them to dominate a niche instead of blending in.
  • Pillars: real estate plus technology and the arts plus Richmond culture.
  • Format and delivery: do what the niche is not doing.
  • Credibility: lead with a unique angle, never a title.
5 levels of differentiation
Hooks Masterclass: Four Horsemen
A hook needs topic clarity and on-target curiosity. It flops from four fixable mistakes: delay, confusion, irrelevance, and disinterest.
  • Delay: lead with the topic in 1 to 2 seconds. Speed to value.
  • Irrelevance: frame with "you/your," not "I/me."
  • Disinterest: open a curiosity loop with contrast, A versus B.
Kallaway hooks masterclass
Elite Communicators: 4 Beats
Hook, Explain, Illustrate, Apply. The backbone that turns rambling into content that lands. This is the exact rhythm the MAMS brand runs on.
  • Hook (3s), Explain like a fifth grader (5 to 8s).
  • Illustrate (20 to 30s) with a story or stat. This is the sauce.
  • Apply: a step, an acronym, or a ratio they can use now.
Framework of elite communicators
Storytelling: First-Person Spiral
Viral story videos run a repeatable shape: mundane sensory open, a reckless yes, escalating complications, a snap twist. A self-aware everyman narrator keeps the loop open the whole way.
  • Open in an ordinary moment with concrete sensory detail.
  • Use real dialogue and named details, not summary.
  • Plant a detail early, pay it off as the twist. This is the cinematic lane.

Death of the Multi-Campaign Funnel
The old top, middle, bottom funnel is obsolete. Collapse it into one campaign, one adset, cold plus warm together, loaded with diverse ads. Meta sequences the awareness stages itself.
  • Stock one adset with problem, solution, and product-aware creatives.

A better way to advertise in 2026
Consolidate Into One Adset
Custom audiences are only suggestions now, so warm and cold overlap. Consolidate to the fewest campaigns and adsets so conversion data concentrates and Meta exits learning fast.
  • One campaign, one adset. 70 conversions beats 7 each across ten.
  • Test locations only. Never test interests or lookalikes.
  • Run 20-plus varied ads per adset. Meta handles it.

Turn Source Into An Idea

How to hand an idea back to me.

When you spot a great angle in the footage or a transcript, do not just say "here is an idea." Run it through these four questions, then fill the template so I can see the loop at a glance.

  1. Find the claim or moment. One line a viewer would argue with or want proof of. The rest of the piece hangs off it.
  2. What question does it raise? If there is no question, it is closed. Rewrite it until a viewer has to stay for the answer.
  3. What is the payoff, and can you hold it? Name the number, spot, or reveal that closes it. If you can't hold it 15 seconds, it is not a loop yet.
  4. Name the archetype. Contrast, smart-operator proof, Courage Arc, origin story, or problem to curiosity. One per idea. It decides where the loops go.
Hand-back template
ANGLE: one line ARCHETYPE: contrast / smart-operator / courage arc / origin / problem to curiosity HOOK LOOP (0-2s): opens Q1 RETENTION LOOP (~15s): opens Q2 before Q1 closes PAYOFF: closes both, in the order opened CLOSE: guide in bio + one engagement question WHY IT HITS: which proven pattern it echoes
