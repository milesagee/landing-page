02

Then I checked it my own way

Where the 2% came from

I did not want to take one dashboard on faith, so I pulled it myself. I drew a band across the Fan and Museum District, roughly Lombardy over to the western edge of the Museum District, Arthur Ashe back to Lombardy, filtered to single-family sold, and compared West Grace inside that band against the rest of it.

The averages came back nearly identical. West Grace around $909,692, the surrounding band around $923,497. On that read the corridor looked like it barely discounted at all, call it two percent. That is the number that did not sit right, and it is exactly what your question forced me to chase down.

03

Same street, 15% and 2% at the same time

Most people pick the number they like and move on. The interesting work is explaining why both are true. Same houses, same fresh 2026 sales, same single-family filter on both sides. The only thing that changes the answer is which yardstick you measure with.

05

The hinge: Grace sells a bigger house

Average finished area, sold comps

A Grace buyer spends about the same money and takes home more house. So the totals match while every foot is cheaper. That is the discount, showing up as space instead of price.

06

How 2% becomes 16%, one decision at a time

Each row changes exactly one choice

Total price cannot even agree with itself here: by average Grace looks slightly cheaper, by median it looks richer. That swing is the signature of a size-driven number. On price per foot, average and median land within three points of each other and point the same way. That agreement is the tell that per-foot is the honest axis.

07

The number I stand behind

Double-sourced

West Grace trades about 15.7 percent under the Fan and Museum band on a price-per-foot basis, roughly $311 a foot against $369, on a full fresh pull of 15 Grace sales against 62 in the band. The first read got 17.6 percent off a handful of hand-picked comps. Two different roads, the same corner. The discount is real. It hides in total price because the money buys more square footage on the busier street.

The method, so you can run it too

How the corridor read is built

  • Fix the ends first. Draw the corridor and the parallel band before pulling a single comp.
  • Hold the filters identical on both sides. Same product, beds, baths, window.
  • Price on dollars per foot, not sticker. Total price tracks size, not value.
  • Median next to mean, then sensitivity-test before you believe it.

What holds up here

  • Direction is stable. Grace trades under the band on every per-foot cut.
  • Two independent samples agree. Hand-picked 17.6, full pull 15.7.
  • Robust to bad rows. Dropping a duplicate record moves it a fifth of a point.
  • Every figure ties to the MLS report footers to the dollar.
