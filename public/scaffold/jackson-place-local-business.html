The move

A competitor can hand a buyer a furniture credit. Nobody can hand them Jackson Ward.

Every Jackson Place buyer closes with The 2nd Street Welcome: roughly $5,000 in credit at the independent businesses within a five minute walk of their front door. Sunday dinner at Mama J's. Breakfast at Salt and Forge. Lunch at Big Herm's. Coffee at Urban Hang Suite. Six of these businesses sit on N. 2nd Street, the same street your ten flagship homes face.

The merchants fund most of it in kind, because a $100 dinner credit costs a kitchen about $30, and because 41 new households arriving one block away is the cheapest customer acquisition they will ever see.

Why a price cut is the worst version of this

On a $536,000 home, five thousand dollars is nine tenths of one percent. It disappears into the closing statement, it compresses your own comps for every later phase, and it changes nobody's mind.

Option A

Cut the price $5,000

Costs you $205,000 off an $18.51M sellout. Invisible to the buyer at 0.9% of price. Differentiates you from nothing, and sets a comp that follows you into every appraisal.

Option B

Buy a furniture or appliance credit

Real cash to a vendor outside the neighborhood. Every competitor can copy it by Friday. It says nothing about where the home actually is.

Option C · The move

Give them the neighborhood

The same $5,000 of felt value for roughly half the cost, and every dollar you do spend lands as revenue in a Jackson Ward register instead of vanishing off your price sheet. Cannot be copied from across the river.

What the merchants actually spend. The in kind portion costs them their margin, not their menu. Restaurants run roughly 30% food cost, so a $100 credit costs about $30. What One South actually does

The idea is the easy part. Signing the cluster, administering the credit and turning it into a demand engine is the work. That is the engagement.

Days 1 to 30

Sign the cluster

Loop in the Historic Jackson Ward Association first, so the package arrives co curated through the partnership you already have. Then sign block by block, starting with the eight on N. 2nd Street. One page in kind agreements. Target 15 signed of 22. The floor covers the gap.

Days 31 to 60

Build the engine

Unique buyer codes, a simple ledger, One South administers and reconciles monthly. Every signed merchant becomes a filmed feature. The Jackson Ward story is the marketing, and the marketing is free. The Welcome book sits in every model home conversation.

Days 61 to 90

Sell against the river

Launch on 2nd Street with the merchants, co hosted with the Association, timed ahead of the Q3 construction start. Then a monthly cadence through sellout, with a redemption report showing you exactly how many dollars landed in the corridor.

Authorize us to go sign them.
