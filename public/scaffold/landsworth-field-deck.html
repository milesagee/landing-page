Lakeside run of show

Ten openers, eight stops,
ninety minutes.

Tuesday, September 1. Lakeside Avenue, Henrico 23228, ending at 7529 Landsworth. Every line below has an open loop, a close that pays it off, and a fact you can defend if somebody screenshots it.

Lewis Ginter is already banked. Do not reshoot it.

Six things that will get you caught

  • Look at the door before you call a business open. Only SB's Love Shack and Lewis Ginter are operator verified inside 90 days. Roy's, Lakeside Tavern, Final Gravity, Revel and the Farmers' Market are all eyes on before you name them.
  • Drive times are modeled, not live. Say "about nine minutes." Never say "Google says."
  • Redfin publishes different windows for different geographies. If you compare 23228 to the county, say the windows out loud, or just stay inside the 23228 numbers.
  • Schools: assignment only, never quality. Holladay Elementary, Moody Middle, Hermitage High. Point people to the HCPS assignment locator and stop talking.
  • 5404 Lakeside is off limits. A fire and an arson charge a named private person was acquitted of. That is not content.
  • The "thirty years of karaoke" number is unverified. If you ask the Segers on camera, get the year on tape.
01 · The openers, ranked
Film these first, in this order. They are selfie takes, front facing, no location dependency past what is named. If the ninety minutes falls apart you still walk away with a video. Shoot each one twice: once static, once walking. Leave three silent seconds at the tail of every take so the editors have a handle.
1

The contradiction

Strongest hookAnywhere on Lakeside Ave
Say it
Henrico County spent two years planning a redesign of this street. In July they voted it out of the plan. And they are still spending five point seven million dollars on it. All three of those are true, and only one of them made the news.
The close
What died on July 28 was the Lakeside corridor redesign inside the HenricoNext 2045 comp plan. The density piece. Townhomes, mixed use, connected sidewalks. What did not die is a separately funded five point seven million dollar capital project called Lakeside Avenue Bike and Pedestrian Improvements. Brook Road to Fairway Avenue. Ten foot shared use path on the north side, five foot sidewalk on the south, traffic calming, a proposed roundabout. Design is underway now, utilities in 2027, construction starts 2028. Board Chair Roscoe Cooper, whose district covers Lakeside, says he is holding town halls to rework the part that got cut. So the street is getting rebuilt either way. The only open question is what gets to be built next to it.
2

Up and coming

Polarity moveRoy's, 5200 Lakeside
Say it
People keep telling me Lakeside is up and coming. There is a burger stand on this street that opened in 1961 and has never closed. Up and coming is just what you call a place the week you found out about it.
The close
Roy's Big Burger, 5200 Lakeside. Opened 1961 by Roy Horne, who had run a What-A-Burger over by the Diamond. Thirty five cent burgers on the opening menu. The Nunnally family bought it when Horne sold and it has stayed in that family since. David Nunnally runs it now. About three hundred burgers a day, seven days a week, off a gas flat top, vegetables cut daily, bread delivered every three days. His line: "We have three generations coming here to eat. I have no plans to retire."

Guess which one sells fastest

The numberAnywhereSay the window
Say it
Median sale price in this zip code is about three hundred fifty thousand. Henrico County as a whole is four hundred. The city of Richmond is about four thirty. Now guess which one of those three sells the fastest.
The close
23228 went pending in about eight days on Redfin's three month window ending May 2026, against ten days a year earlier. Compete score 91. Sale to list 101.7 percent in May. Henrico County sat at twenty eight days on its December 2025 window. The cheapest of the three is the fastest of the three. 5

The one that lasted

SB's, 6935 LakesideVerified open
Say it
This restaurant opened here in 2017. Opened a second one in the Fan in 2022. Closed the Fan one in 2024. Kept this one. That is not a restaurant story. That is a real estate story, and I will tell you what it says about this street.
The close
SB's Lakeside Love Shack, 6935 Lakeside. Opened November 2017. Sarabeth Hagen owns it, cooks it, writes the specials board, and lives in Lakeside. The whole thing is B-52's themed. Her site opens with "Well, it's set way back in the middle of Lakeside." The Shack Stacks and the TJ Shimmie are named after her son TJ. The Main Street location ran February 2022 to March 2024. Why the Lakeside one lasted is your read, not a statistic. Say "that is my read" out loud when you give it.
6

Not named after a lake

AnywhereBest comment bait
Say it
Lakeside is not named after a lake. It is named after a bicycle club. And the clubhouse is still standing about a mile from where I am right now.
The close
In 1895 Lewis Ginter named his bicycle club the Lakeside Wheel Club. That clubhouse is Bloemendaal House, the one at the botanical garden. He built Lakeside Park on the grounds that are now Jefferson Lakeside Country Club, and people took a streetcar out to it when it had a zoo, a bowling alley and a dance pavilion. The whole neighborhood is named after a rich man's weekend hobby.
7

Two hundred sixty two acres

Bryan Park
Say it
There are two hundred sixty two acres of park at the end of this street, and most of Richmond drives past the entrance at seventy miles an hour without ever knowing it is there.
The close
Joseph Bryan Park. I-95, I-64 and 195 all converge at the Bryan Park interchange, which is exactly why nobody stops. The azalea garden inside it is seventeen acres, established 1952 out of five thousand donated azaleas, peaked above fifty thousand plants, and pulled as many as four hundred thousand visitors a spring in the sixties and seventies. The city stopped maintaining it in the eighties. Volunteers from Friends of Bryan Park brought it back, and the city is now doing dam removal and stream restoration on Upham Brook, approved by Planning Commission in April.
8

Nobody spends that on a neighborhood they are leaving

Uses banked Ginter footageBuyer case
Say it
The botanical garden at the end of this road just finished a thirty one million dollar expansion. Then it turned around and bought six more acres from the church next door. Nobody spends that on a neighborhood they are planning to leave.
The close
Lewis Ginter's THRIVE campaign, thirty one million dollar goal, twenty six million raised as of the garden's own October 2025 page. The expansion opened to the public on May 23 of this year. Seven and a half acres of new gardens, Wick Lake brought inside the grounds, a bigger conservatory with four climate zones, and a permanent year round butterfly house. The 6.2 acres came from Lakeside Baptist Church and ownership transferred in July.
9

The dive bar

Lakeside Tavern, 5406Status not verified, look at the door
Say it
There is a dive bar on this street with a sign so faded you can barely read it, and the owner's mother cooks Korean food in the back. I did not believe it either.
The close
Lakeside Tavern, 5406 Lakeside. James Seger bartended there before he and his mother Kye bought it from the previous owners. He runs the front, she runs the kitchen. Bulgogi, bibimbap, and kkanpunggi style wings. His line: "She loves to cook, and she wanted to incorporate some of her style into Lakeside. We serve whatever my mother feels like." Karaoke is documented. The number of years it has been running is not, so do not put a number on it unless they say it to your camera.
10

Somebody's wife said it would make a good market

Farmers' Market, 6110The heart line
Say it
In 2005 this was an asphalt parking lot with a business picnic on it, and somebody's wife said it would make a good farmers market. Six years later the owners sank twenty foot posts six feet into concrete and said this thing will be here in a hundred years. I am telling you that on a real estate page for a reason.
The close
Lakeside Farmers' Market, 6110 Lakeside. Opened 2007 as Henrico County's first farmers market. Peter and Sharon Francisco own it and their own site calls them Northsiders since birth. It was Betty Thornton, an extension agent and the wife of the Fairfield supervisor at the time, who told Sharon the lot would make a wonderful market. The 2011 pavilion cost almost half a million dollars. Peter's line: "This market will be here in a hundred years." Day one vendors are still selling there. That is what a neighborhood with a floor under it looks like, and a floor is the whole reason I am comfortable putting a buyer here.
