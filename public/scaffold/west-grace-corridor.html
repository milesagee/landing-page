West Grace St · Upper Fan & Museum District edge

The Fan's busy street, priced like it.

West Grace trades at a real, measurable discount to the quiet parallel avenues around it. Here is the number, the comps behind it, why it happens, and the method so you can run it on any corridor in Richmond next week.

The verdict

Grace runs about 15 to 18 percent under matched Fan streets on a single-family basis, and roughly 20 percent under the median on 2 to 4 unit product.

Single-family base case: a West Grace median of $314/sqft against a matched comparison median of $381/sqft (Stuart, Franklin, Park, Kensington), a $67/sqft gap and a 17.6% discount. It holds under sensitivity, narrowing to ~15.6% if the low Grove outlier is included. The direction is stable; the public sold sample is thin, so treat it as a working underwriting range, not an appraisal.

Single-family lens

The buyer pays less for an otherwise similar house on a higher-throughput through-street. Street function is the lead driver.

Multi-family lens

Per-door income value holds (2603 Grace ~$292k/door, near the 75th pct) while whole-building price sits at the 25th. The missing money is the owner-occupant premium.

04

Why Grace discounts

Ranked · labeled evidence vs. inference
1

Through-route / corridor function

Grace is one of the Fan's main east-west thoroughfares; the comparison set is quiet parallel streets (Stuart, Park, Kensington). Buyers pay less for otherwise-similar houses on higher-throughput streets. Largest driver.

Provable function
2

Traffic conflict & street design

One-way-to-two-way conversion history and higher crash-conflict on Grace than Franklin in the discussed segment. Grace is perceived and experienced differently than the side streets.

Partially supported
3

Parking pressure near the VCU edge

Extended permit hours on the 1600 and 1800 blocks of W Grace vs. standard Fan limits. Explains the eastern edge; not the whole 2000–3300 corridor equally.

Partially supported
4

VCU proximity & rental-conversion density

Dense off-campus rental supply, mixed-use labeling, on-corridor multifamily (2607, 3230 W Grace). Shifts buyer expectations on occupancy mix, parking, condition. This is the lead driver on the multifamily basis.

Partially supported
5

Commercial & mixed-use edges

Parts of the broader Grace corridor read as mixed-use, unlike the purely residential parallel streets. Secondary, because the 2000–3300 range still holds strong residential blocks.

Partially supported
6

Lot geometry & condition mix

Grace included comps skew larger or renovated yet still cluster in the low $300s/sf. A sample-composition warning that may understate the like-for-like discount, not a proven independent driver.

Inferred
05

The method: measuring any corridor discount

The transferable product. The number is the byproduct.
Draw the ends firstFix a start and end block before pulling comps. Stop where the product changes, not where the street name does.
Name the comparison setNearby streets a buyer would cross-shop. Share era, product type, size band, buyer profile.
State what is held constantGeography, type, era, size, recency held. Let the corridor variable move (traffic, parking, edges, conversion).
Sold comps, not listings12-month window if possible, 24 if thin (label it). Listings are context, not evidence.
Two tables before any mathSubject corridor and comparison set. Address, date, price, sqft, $/sf, beds/baths, lot, condition, include/exclude.
Normalize by $/sfPrice / finished sqft each row. Same finished-area convention across sources.
Flag distortions firstNew build, condos, multifamily, estate/non-arms-length, teardowns, heavy reno, very small/large, missing data.
Median, not averageHarder for one weird sale to bend. Always report the range alongside it.
Discount two waysSubtract subject median from comparison median; divide that gap by the comparison median for the percent.
Run one sensitivity testAdd back flagged-but-plausible sales. If the number survives, it is usable. If it swings, the sample is fragile.
Split per-door vs. whole-buildingOn income product: if per-door holds but whole-building lags, the discount is a missing buyer, not weak income.
Label every driverProvable, Partially supported, or Inferred. The math proves the spread; drivers explain it separately.
06

Confidence, sensitivity & the gaps

What holds up

  • Direction is stable. Grace trades below the matched set under every cut.
  • Sensitivity survives. $58 to $67/sf, 15% to 18%. Adding back reno comps barely moves the Grace median.
  • Two bases agree. Single-family (15–18%) and 2-4 unit (~20%) point the same way.

What to tighten before pricing a deal

  • Sample is thin. Under ~5 clean comps a side. Working range, not appraisal-grade.
  • No causal isolation. The table proves the spread; it does not split traffic vs. parking vs. use vs. condition mathematically.
  • Next move: full MLS export + assessor-transfer reconciliation for the 2000–3400 band, code condition by hand, re-run.
