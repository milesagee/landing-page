You are not fighting each other.
You are pulling the same door in opposite directions.

Joseph ends a fight when he is understood. Gina ends a fight when it stops. Both of those are correct. They just cannot both happen at the same moment, and that collision is what the yelling actually is.

So this page does not ask either of you to be less of who you are. It gives you a way to both get what you need, in an order that works.

Flooded right now? Start here. Three steps. Nothing to decide. Nothing to read.
In the moment

The three steps.

This is the whole thing. Everything below this card is just the reasoning.

01

Say the word.

Either one of you can call it. Calling it means "I love you and we are flooded." It never means "I win."

When it gets said, you both stop mid sentence.

No last word. Not one. The last word is where every single fight restarts. Whoever is still talking after the word is breaking the agreement, and that is the only rule here with no exceptions.
Make it ridiculous. It is supposed to be hard to say angrily. It saves here and shows up everywhere else on this page and on both of your own pages.

02

Twenty minutes. Apart.

Joseph goes to the garage or downstairs. Gina goes to her room (see below, it is being built).

Hands busy. Do not rehearse the argument in your head. Rehearsing it is the same as having it.

Twenty minutes is not a mood. It is roughly how long a body actually takes to come back down once it is flooded. Coming back at minute four is coming back still lit.

Now come back

The pause is over. It does not end here.

A break that just ends is not a break. It is walking away, and walking away is the thing that actually breaks a marriage. So you come back. You do not have to fix it. You just have to show up.

"I'm back. Do you want to docket this, or are we good?"

That is the entire re entry. Two options, and both of them are allowed.

03

Say "docket."

Either one of you says the word docket and the topic gets a real time slot. Within 48 hours, or the standing weekly one.

Saying it is the whole transaction. No preview. No summary. No "let me just say one thing."

A docket is not a shelf. A shelf is where a thing goes to be forgotten and then leak out three weeks later. A docket has a court date. That difference is the entire agreement, and it is what lets Joseph stop without losing the topic.

02
The trade

The fight ends now. And nothing gets buried.

Here is the deal, and it only works because each of you is protected by the other one's need. That is what makes it hold.

Gina gets the end. When the word is said, it is over. Immediately. Not negotiated, not "one more thing." Over.

Joseph gets the guarantee. The topic is not gone. It has a time. It gets finished, all the way, with him being heard, and it is not going to reopen six weeks later because it never actually closed.

Gina, you can only ask him to stop because you are promising it comes back. Joseph, you can only stop because she is promising it comes back.

Take either promise away and the whole thing collapses. That is why you both sign it.

03
The hearing

Understanding is not agreeing.

This is where the docketed thing actually gets closed. 25 minutes, on the clock. The clock protects Gina. The fact that it happens at all protects Joseph.

One of you talks. The other one says it back in their own words. Then you ask: did I get it?

And you keep going until the person who was talking says the two words that end it: "that's it."

Then you swap.

Saying it back is not agreeing. You can restate his whole point perfectly and still think he is completely wrong. That is allowed. That is the point.

Being understood is the finish line. Winning is not. If either of you is still trying to win at minute 24, the hearing is not working and you need to say so out loud.

Close it one of two ways. Either there is one concrete thing that changes, or the honest outcome is "nothing changes, I just needed you to know." That second one is a complete result. It is not a consolation prize.

And then it is closed. Joseph, in your words: the chapter got read to the end. It does not get reopened without something new. That is Gina's protection, and you owe it to her because she gave you the hearing.

04
When your mother is here

The man who built this house does not have to raise his voice in it.

Joseph, this one is yours, and Gina named the fix before I did. The volume is not what makes you the head of this house. It is the thing that costs you the room.

You said it yourself: it starts at the very beginning, before you can catch it. So the work is not staying calm for an hour. It is one breath, before the first sentence. One beat. Then you already know what to say, because your wife wrote it for you.

Strike one "Mom, this is my house. I want you to respect me. Let's leave it at that." Strike two "Mom, I asked you to stop." Then Take it outside, away from the kids. Or you leave the room.

Gina wrote this. Out loud, in one take, without being asked. She was not telling you what you did wrong. She was handing you the exit you could not find while you were in it.

She may not stop. She may go right past strike two, every time, for the rest of her life. That is a real possibility and this protocol is built to survive it.

So hear the important part: walking out after strike two is not losing. It is the win. A good attorney does not out shout the witness. He rests his case and lets the record speak.

The goal was never to make her stop. The goal is that you stay steady. That is entirely yours, and nobody can take it.

One guardrail, and it matters. The wit is aimed at the situation, or at yourself. Never at her. A clever line aimed at your mother is still a weapon, it is just a better dressed one.

Gina, this one is optional. It is a gift, not a duty.

When it starts, he is fighting on two fronts and he is doing it alone. A touch. A look. The code word said soft. Anything that means I see it, I'm with you.

A piece of that volume is loneliness. One signal takes it out of the room.

07
Your call, not mine

Can the kids call the word?

There is a real argument for yes, and it is not a small one. But this changes their role in the house, so it is yours to decide, together, at the table.

Right now, when it gets loud, your son has one option: step in and referee. He has already done it. And it put him in a position no kid should have, standing between his father and his grandmother, trying to hold the line by himself.

If he can call the code word, that changes. He is not a cop anymore. He is a person in the family with a sanctioned, slightly ridiculous flag he is allowed to throw. And the grown ups have to stop, because they agreed to.

The case against

It gives a kid power over adult conflict.

It could get used to dodge things he does not want to hear. It could make him feel responsible for regulating the house, which is not his job.

Both of those are real. Which is exactly why this is a decision you two make out loud, and not one I make for you.

One plain thing, said once. This is a tool that two people who love each other agreed to try. It is not therapy and it does not pretend to be. If the anger ever starts to feel bigger than a tool, that is a different conversation, and it is a worthy one.

The agreement

Not because a signature is binding. Because you cannot negotiate the rules in the middle of a fight, and the whole point is that these already exist before the next one starts.
