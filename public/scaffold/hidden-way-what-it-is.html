1 What the listing is

A floor plan on a lot, not a finished home

This listing is to be built on any lot in the subdivision map. Photos are of the same floor plan built elsewhere. Public remarks, CVRMLS 2623358, listed August 26, 2026. Read September 9, 2026.

Three separate fields on the sheet say the same thing. Year built reads 2026 / To Be Built. Occupant reads TO BE BUILT. Annual taxes read $0, which is what a parcel shows when there is no structure on it to assess yet.

The owner of record is Hidden Rock Sbr Owner, LLC, a corporate entity, and possession comes at closing. In plain terms, you would not be buying a house from a homeowner. You would be signing a build contract with a builder who currently owns the land underneath it.

That is not a problem in itself. Plenty of good homes start exactly this way. It is a problem if you walked in thinking you could tour it Saturday, because the house in the photographs is standing somewhere else.

5 Before you act

Four things I want in writing first

The incentive number, reconciled

The MLS says up to $20,000. Schell's own site says up to $40,000. I want the current incentive sheet with a date on it, and I want to know which of the three pieces you actually qualify for and what each one is tied to. Some builder incentives require using their affiliated lender, and that condition is where the real cost usually hides.

The square footage, broken out

The remarks describe a beautifully finished basement. The sheet lists 3,850 square feet above grade and zero below grade finished. Both cannot be true as written. Either the basement finish got counted above grade, which would inflate the number your price per foot is built on, or the basement is finished space that is not in the 3,850 at all, which would mean you are getting more than the sheet says. I want it itemized before anyone prices anything. The sheet already flags the source as per builder, not a measurement.

The option list that gets you from $734,900 to $936,075

Line by line, with the lot premium separated from the structural options and those separated from the finish selections. Structural choices are locked early and cost the most to change. Finish selections are where a build contract quietly grows after you sign.

The written warranty, and the build timeline with dates

Schell does not publish warranty terms online. Referenced documentation mentions a ten year limited structural warranty and a one year installation warranty, but I have not seen the document itself and I am not going to describe coverage I have not read. Ask for the actual warranty booklet, plus a build schedule with a delivery window and what happens if it slips.

6 Honest limits

What I could not check yet, and why

  • Goochland County permits and inspections for this builder. The county's permit records are not queryable from outside. This is being pulled by hand and it is the single most useful thing still outstanding.
  • Virginia state court and lien filings. The state court portal prohibits automated searching, so Goochland and Chesterfield civil filings have to be run manually. In progress.
  • Contractor board disciplinary history. The DPOR lookup sits behind a challenge page. The license number and classifications are confirmed. Whether the board has ever acted on it is not, yet.
  • How much of Hidden Rock is actually built. Forty lots were platted. I do not yet know how many are sold, under construction, or standing, and that changes what living there looks like for the next two years.
  • What the finished Schell homes nearby have resold for. Goochland new construction closings in the $700K to $1M band are being pulled so we can see whether this price sits inside the market or outside it.
