Module 01

The interment log

The cheapest thing on this page and the one everything else depends on. Right now, twenty to a hundred people stand on your ground and then disappear without a trace.

What to capture
One page, filled out the same day, every time

This is not a database project and it does not need software. A single sheet per interment, filed with the deed record.

The fields

  • Deceased, date, section and space
  • The purchasing family member, with phone and address
  • Every other family member present who signed the register, with relationship
  • What adjoins that space and whether it is available
  • Which funeral home handled it and which director specifically
  • Whether burial or cremation, and what happened to the cremated remains

That last field matters more than it looks. If cremation families are walking off your property with an urn, you are watching revenue leave and you have no record of how much.

Why the register alone is not enough

A signature book tells you names. It does not tell you relationships, and the relationship is what makes the adjacency conversation land. The person who needs to hear about the space next door is the surviving spouse or the eldest child, not the coworker who came to pay respects.

Module 02

The adjacency conversation

The highest converting sales moment in the business, and the easiest one to destroy by getting the timing wrong.

The rule that protects everything Never at the funeral. Never at the graveside. Never in the week of the death. The reputation that built this business over 68 years is worth more than any single sale, and families talk. One story about the cemetery that sold to them at the casket would cost you more than a year of adjacency revenue.
The timing
The memorial setting appointment, roughly 30 to 60 days out

By then the family is already sitting with you, already making decisions about the space, and already thinking about permanence. The conversation is natural there in a way it can never be at the service.

How it opens

What the office says "While you're here, one thing I want you to know rather than find out later. The two spaces next to your mother are still open. Families sometimes want to keep everyone together and they only think of it years on, when it's already gone. I'm not asking you to decide anything today. I just didn't want you to not know."

Why this works and a pitch does not

  • It is framed as information, not an offer, which is what it actually is.
  • It names the real risk, which is that the space gets sold to a stranger.
  • It explicitly removes the pressure to decide, which is the only reason the family can hear it.
  • It is true. That is the whole reason it lands.

What to have ready

A simple diagram of that section showing what is taken and what is open, and today's price. If someone has to go look it up, the moment is gone.

The compounding effect

Every additional space sold to a family already buried on your ground is margin on land you have already cleared, sold to a buyer you already have, at no acquisition cost whatsoever.

Module 03

The memorial cadence

Markers get ordered months after the burial, and a meaningful share never get ordered at all. Not because the family decided against it. Because nobody followed up and grief is disorganizing.

Four touches
Scheduled the day of the interment, not remembered later
30
Days. Care call only. No business. Confirm the space is settled and ask if anything needs attention.
90
Days. Memorial options, with the section diagram. This is the adjacency conversation too.
180
Days. If no marker ordered, a soft check. Many families are waiting for permission, not information.
365
Days. The anniversary. A note that you remembered. Nothing sold on this one, ever.

The one at 30 days is not a sales call

It is the reason the other three are welcome. A cemetery that calls only when it wants an order is a vendor. A cemetery that calls to ask how the family is doing is the place they send their relatives. That difference is the entire business you have already built, and this just makes it deliberate instead of accidental.

The 365 rule

Nothing is ever offered on the anniversary call. If the family raises something, take it. If they do not, the call ends. Breaking this once teaches every family that the anniversary note was a sales tactic, and you cannot get that back.

Module 04

The director accounts

You already have the relationships. What you do not have is a count, and you cannot grow what you are not measuring.

Turn a relationship into an account
Every funeral home gets a name, a number, and a rhythm

The standing list

  • Funeral home name, and the specific director you deal with. Not the business. The person.
  • Families sent this year, and last year. This single column will tell you things you do not currently know.
  • Date of last contact.
  • Whether they operate their own crematory. This becomes the wholesale list the moment your retort is running.

The rhythm

  • Same day response, always. A director calling you has a family sitting in their office. Whoever answers first wins, and it is frequently that simple.
  • Current price and availability sheet in every director's hands, reissued whenever prices move. A director who has to call to get a number will sometimes just quote a competitor instead.
  • Quarterly in person. Not a pitch. Fifteen minutes and a face. This is the entire relationship in most cases.
Do not do this Do not offer anything of value in exchange for referrals. Referral compensation in deathcare carries real regulatory exposure and it would put a 68-year license and reputation at risk for marginal volume. The channel is won on responsiveness, price transparency, and the property looking the way it looks. That is enough.

What the count unlocks

Once you can see that three houses send you sixty percent of your families and eleven houses send almost none, you know exactly where the next quarter goes. Right now that picture exists only in memory, which means it is probably wrong in at least one expensive direction.

Module 05

The deed book revival

You are sitting on 68 years of buyers. Most cemeteries never touch this file and it is the closest thing to free revenue in the business.

Work it in batches
Start with the largest partially filled family sections

Do not start at 1958 and work forward. Start where the money is: families who bought multiple spaces, used some, and left the rest empty. They have already proven they buy, they already have people buried there, and they may not remember what they own.

The letter

What it says "Our records show your family holds four spaces in the Garden of Peace, two of which are still available. I wanted to make sure you knew, since these records go back decades and families sometimes lose track. If you'd like the current section map, or if anything in our records looks wrong, please call."

No price. No offer. Nothing to decline. It is a records notification, which is precisely what it is, and it generates calls because people genuinely want to know.

The second pass

Families who bought a single space and never came back. Lower yield, much larger volume. Worth running once the first batch is worked.

The inventory question underneath it

Working this file will surface abandoned and unclaimed spaces in old sections. Those are real inventory sitting on your books that you may be able to recover and resell. Virginia has a process for this, and it is worth knowing exactly where you stand before you need to. Bring your attorney in on this one rather than working from what anyone tells you it is.

Module 06

The section relationships

Slowest to start, biggest per win. One relationship can move fifty to two hundred spaces, and then keep moving them for a generation without anyone selling anything.

Who to approach
Organizations whose members already want to be near each other
  • Churches without their own burial ground. The largest and most natural fit. Many congregations have wanted a section for years and never had anyone offer.
  • Fraternal orders and lodges. Long institutional memory, and a tradition of collective burial.
  • Cultural and national associations. Communities that want to be buried together, often underserved by existing cemeteries.
  • Veterans posts, with the caveat below.

The veterans caveat

A qualifying veteran can be buried at no cost in a national or state veterans cemetery, which is genuine competition you cannot price against. The opening is narrower and specific: veterans who want to be buried near family rather than in a veterans cemetery, and spouses whose eligibility is not what families assume. Learn the eligibility rules precisely before approaching a post, because getting this wrong in front of that audience is unrecoverable.

How the offer is structured

A defined, named section reserved for the organization, at a group rate, with spaces sold to members individually over time. The organization is not buying inventory and taking risk. It is being given a home, which is what it actually wants.

Why it compounds without effort

Once twenty members of a congregation are buried in one section, the rest of that congregation follows on its own. The section sells itself from that point forward, and it does it for decades. This is the lever that is still producing revenue long after everything else on this page has been optimized.

The whole playbook in one line

Sell the ground to the living, treat the family better than anyone else would, and let the rest of the revenue come to you at the prices you charge on the day it happens.

Which is what you have been doing for 68 years. This just writes it down so it does not have to live in your head, and so it keeps running on the days you are not there.
