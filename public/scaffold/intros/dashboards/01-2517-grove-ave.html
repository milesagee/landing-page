The honest read

None of these cash flow.

What the numbers actually say

This is priced like an appreciation play, not an income play.

$1.15M against $63K of annual rent works out to a 2.6% cap rate. Fan multifamily typically trades 4.5 to 5.5%. The seller priced this for an owner-occupant or a long-term appreciation buyer, not for someone who needs the building to feed itself.

Real out-of-pocket on the recommended Scenario C, after factoring in maintenance and vacancy reserves on a 115-year-old building, is closer to $3,800 per month. That's roughly $46,000 a year Dad would be feeding the property.

To break even monthly at asking, the down payment would need to be roughly $632,000 (55%). At that point the cash-on-cash return is effectively zero. Same as a savings account, except you can't pull the money out and the building can leak.

Three angles to make it work

Where the deal actually opens up.

If we want this property to make sense as a long-term hold, here's where to push.

01

Negotiate the price down.

The seller is a licensed Realtor and the assessment is $905K. A $950K offer drops Scenario C's monthly burn to about $1,328. A $900K offer brings it to about $1,067. Doesn't get us to positive cash flow, but cuts the bleed roughly in half. The $245K gap above assessment is a real anchor for an offer conversation.

02

Plan around the 2027 rent reset.

Current rents are well below Fan market. The 2-bedrooms at $1,465 should be $1,650-1,950. The 1-bedroom at $875 should be $1,000-1,250. When the leases roll in May/June 2027, gross rent could lift to roughly $6,400 a month, or $76,800 a year. That's about $1,150 a month of extra income, which puts Scenario C close to break-even monthly. We'd write checks for about 14 months, then the building feeds itself.

03

Stack tax credits that bypass the passive loss rules.

This is the creative layer. At Dad's income, the obvious tax benefits (depreciation, mortgage interest deductions) get suspended. But federal tax credits work differently. They flow directly against W2 income with no passive loss limit. The six stacks below are where the real unlock lives.

The tax stack

Six creative angles, ranked by real impact.

Tap any stack to see the mechanics. The top three bypass passive loss rules entirely, which is what makes them work for an above-$150K AGI investor.

Stack 01 · Cleanest unlock

Federal Solar Investment Tax Credit

$9,900–$12,600
Direct W2 credit
› How it works

A 30 percent federal credit on the cost of a solar installation. For a building this size, a 12 kW system runs $33-42K installed, which yields a $9,900-$12,600 credit straight against Dad's W2 tax bill in year one.

Why it bypasses the passive loss trap: Per IRS Notice 2023-17, the Solar ITC is not subject to passive activity loss limits. It flows directly against W2 tax liability. If Dad's tax bill in year one can't absorb the full credit, IRA §6418 lets him sell the unused portion to a corporate buyer at about 92 cents on the dollar.

Virginia layer: Property tax exemption on the system is automatic. Sales tax exemption on equipment saves another $1,800-$2,200. Annual electricity savings are around $1,956 and Virginia SREC revenue adds about $396 a year as portfolio income (also not passive).

Stack 02 · The big one

Historic Tax Credit (HTC)

$30K–$60K
Over 5 years
› How it works

A 20 percent federal credit on qualified rehabilitation expenses for certified historic structures. 2517 Grove is a 1910 brick building in The Fan, which is National Register-eligible. The credit applies to capital improvements (roof, HVAC, electrical, plumbing, windows, insulation) that Dad would be spending on anyway across the first few years.

Example math: A $150K rehab generates a $30K credit, ratable at $6K a year for 5 years. A $300K rehab generates $60K, at $12K a year. Both bypass passive loss limits.

The catch: Requires Virginia State Historic Preservation Office (SHPO) approval on Part 1 (description) and Part 2 (rehab plan) before work begins. Adds 6-10 weeks to project timing. Retroactive approval is rare. Plan for it from the start or this credit walks.
Stack 03 · Tenant amenity + credit

EV charging credit (§30C)

$2,400–$3,600
Year 1 credit
› How it works

30 percent federal credit on the cost of installing EV chargers on the property, up to $30K per location. Install 2-4 chargers on the 4 off-street parking spaces. Cost runs $8-12K, so the credit lands at $2,400-$3,600 directly against W2 tax.

Also bypasses passive loss rules. Bonus: tenant amenity that future-proofs the property and likely supports rent growth at the 2027 reset.

Stack 04 · Absorbs trapped losses

Generate passive income elsewhere

Frees $20–45K
Of trapped losses
› How it works

Passive losses can offset passive income with no AGI cap. If Dad buys a cash-flowing passive investment (a multifamily syndication, oil and gas working interest, or a dividend partnership), Grove's suspended losses become useful instead of just sitting on the return.

Example: $200K in a syndication distributing $18K/yr of passive income. Grove generates $25K/yr of passive losses. The $18K of syndication income gets fully shielded, and the rest of Grove's losses still carry forward. Net portfolio tax bill drops materially without changing anything about Grove itself.

This is the right pairing if Dad is open to deploying capital into a second income stream that gives the Grove losses a job to do.

Stack 05 · Worth checking

Spousal REPS (the override)

Unlimited
Against W2 income
› How it works

Real Estate Professional Status under IRC §469(c)(7) makes ALL rental losses non-passive on a joint return. If Mom (or whoever the joint filer is) can credibly log 750+ hours a year materially participating in real estate AND more hours in real estate than in any other trade or business, every dollar of paper loss flows against Dad's W2 income immediately.

The qualification bar: Requires someone in the household who doesn't have another full-time W2 job. The 750 hours can cover managing this property plus others (acquisition research, tenant communication, repair coordination, books, planning).

Mom, this is worth a conversation if you're open to it. It would be the single biggest unlock on the deal. Even partial qualification with documented hours changes the picture materially. Worth flagging to the CPA either way.

Stack 06 · Conditional

Cost segregation + bonus depreciation

$20K–$30K
Only if Stack 4 or 5 lands
› Read the catch first

A cost segregation study on a $1.15M property typically reclassifies 25-35 percent of the depreciable basis to 5/7/15-year property. Combined with 2026 bonus depreciation, year-one paper deduction lands at $65-95K. At a 32 percent marginal bracket, that's a theoretical $20.8-30.4K tax benefit.

Study fee: $6,500-$10,500.

The catch: This generates a deduction, not a credit. At Dad's AGI it gets trapped by passive loss rules. Only worth running the study if Stack 4 (passive income to absorb it) or Stack 5 (spousal REPS) is in place. Otherwise it just makes the suspended loss pile bigger.

One nuance worth confirming with the CPA: OBBBA appears to have restored 100 percent bonus depreciation for property acquired after 1/19/2025, which would dramatically change the year-one numbers. The TCJA phase-out schedule says 20 percent for 2026. Practitioners are split on which applies. Get a firm read before committing.
