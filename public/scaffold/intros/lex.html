01 · Leave clean

Virginia changed the non-compete law this summer.

Since July 1, 2026, an employer in Virginia can't enter into, enforce, or threaten to enforce a non-compete against a health care professional. The law's definition names anyone licensed, registered, or certified by the Board of Social Work.

The catch is one date

The new ban only covers non-competes entered into, amended, or renewed on or after July 1, 2026. Older contracts fall under the old rules. So the first thing to find is the date on your contract, and any renewal or amendment since.

Signed or renewed on or after July 1, 2026You're likely covered by the ban. Confirm that it applies to you as a supervisee.
Likely void
Signed before July 1, 2026The old rules apply. Courts read it strictly against the employer, and won't rewrite an overbroad clause into a valid one.
Fact fight
Earn under $1,507/week, or you're paid overtimeA separate "low-wage" ban. It applies no matter when you signed.
Void

What still holds either way

  • Client non-solicitation. The law still allows a clause that stops you from reaching out to her clients. It protects you when a client finds you on her own and you didn't initiate contact. It doesn't let you contact them.
  • Training repayment. For health care workers there less than 5 years, the law allows prorated repayment of training, recruitment, or bonus costs. This is probably the "money I owe her" piece.
  • The records. They stay with the practice. Clients can request their own records, and the practice has 30 days to respond.

Her breaches: real, but not automatic

Under Virginia law (Horton v. Horton), the party who commits the first material breach can't enforce the contract. "Material" is a fact question, and we found no Virginia case using it to void a non-compete. That makes the breaches leverage, not a guarantee. The time to document them is now, while you're still there.

Before you give notice

Protect yourself in this order.

1 · The dateFind when your contract was signed, and every renewal or amendment since, including pay changes and title changes.
2 · Your fileKeep copies of your own contract, pay stubs, and a dated record of each breach. Only your own records. Nothing about any client, ever.
3 · SilenceSay nothing to clients about your practice yet. No lists, no numbers, no "find me later."
4 · One hour with an attorneyA Virginia employment attorney, before notice. Bring the questions below.
5 · At noticeThe NASW Code (1.17f) says to tell clients their options for continuing care, and the benefits and risks of each. Get the exact wording approved by your attorney first.
6 · AfterWhen a former client finds you on her own, record in her intake how she found you. That note is your proof.

This is research to prep you for the attorney, not legal advice. We don't need to see your contract, and neither does anyone except your lawyer.

02 · The machine

Nine steps. Five run themselves.

This is the whole journey, from a stranger's search to a weekly client, built in SimplePractice. Each step is marked as automatic or yours. The steps marked yours total about 20 minutes per new client, plus the consult.

  1. She finds you Build onceGoogle Business Profile, your search pages, Therapy Finder, or a referral from the map below.
  2. She requests a free consult AutoSet the free 15-minute consult as the only service new clients can book online. They can't book a full session until you've talked. Every request lands on your Inquiries page as a prospective client.
  3. You accept 2 minSimplePractice makes you approve each request. Check twice a day, between sessions. Same-day replies are what win the client.
  4. Reminders go out AutoEmail, text, or voice, 24 to 72 hours before. Clients can confirm or cancel from the text. In-person reminders need Essential or higher, which is why Starter isn't enough.
  5. The consult 15 minFour things: is it a fit, your rate said out loud, the commitment (weekly, for months), and "what makes now the right time."
  6. Intake, one click 5 minMark your intake forms as defaults so they're pre-selected. One click sends them with the portal invite. The Good Faith Estimate is a form you fill in and sign, so build a template and copy it. Her card goes on file.
  7. Weekly slot, set once AutoBook her first session as a weekly recurring series, up to 100 appointments. You never schedule her again.
  8. Billing AutoTurn on daily auto-invoicing plus AutoPay, and her card is charged overnight after each session. Late cancels and no-shows are billed the full fee automatically, based on the window you set. Put that policy in your consent forms.
  9. When you're full AutoNew inquiries go to the waitlist (Essential or higher). The day a slot opens, you already know who's next.

Why private-pay practices stall

This is a read on the pattern, not a study. A struggling practice usually has one of four gaps. Each one has a fix below.

  • Generic. "Anxiety, depression, trauma, couples, teens." Nobody feels like it was written for her.
  • Price hidden. The consult gets spent on the fee conversation, and the wrong people keep booking consults.
  • Slow reply. She finally works up the courage to reach out. Two days go by. She books with whoever answered first.

Your concierge month, rebuilt

Your instinct is right: up to 8 sessions a month for women who want to go deep. Researchers are testing twice-weekly EMDR for childhood-trauma PTSD, so the idea has real clinical backing. Two changes protect you:

  • Don't discount the hour. Present it as a twice-weekly track when it's clinically right for her, not as a bulk deal. 8 sessions at $175 is $1,400 a month.
  • Refund unused sessions, prorated. If paid-for sessions just expire, it can start to look legally like you're selling insurance. A prorated refund keeps you clean and keeps her trust.
  • Frame it as a phase, not forever. Stabilize first, then run a 6 to 8 week deep-work phase. That's a clear start and finish she can say yes to.

04 · Where AI fits

AI does the marketing. You do the healing.

There's a hard line, and it's simple. AI can write about your practice. It never sees a client.

Yes, all day

  • Drafting your website pages, directory profiles, and "what to expect" page.
  • Blog posts that answer real search questions, like "what does an EMDR session feel like."
  • Instagram captions and generic email templates with no client details.
  • You review every clinical claim before it goes live. AI gets things confidently wrong.

Never

  • A client's name, dates, diagnosis, or anything from a session in ChatGPT. The regular consumer version has no HIPAA agreement.
  • An AI chatbot as the first voice a trauma survivor meets. That first contact is you, or a form that goes straight to you.
  • Meta or Google ad tracking code on your booking or intake pages.

05 · Before the door opens

Four rules that protect the license you just earned.

Until the license issuesVirginia's title for you right now is "Supervisee in Social Work," and supervisees can't present themselves as independent practitioners. Build the whole site now, but keep it unpublished until you have your LCSW number. Call the Board of Social Work and confirm the timing.
Good Faith EstimateFederal law requires you to give every self-pay client a written cost estimate, within 1 to 3 business days of booking depending on how far out the appointment is. Post the "Right to a Good Faith Estimate" notice on your Fees page.
TestimonialsNever ask a current client for a testimonial. The NASW Code prohibits it. Your credibility on the site comes from your training, your approach, and your words.
Prepaid sessionsUnused sessions get refunded, prorated. Always.

None of this is legal advice. It's the checklist to take to the Board, and to an attorney or NASW Virginia if anything feels unclear.
