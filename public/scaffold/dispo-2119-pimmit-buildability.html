02 What it means in plain terms

The home dates to 1953, so it is a legally established pre-RPA structure, and about 89% of its footprint sits inside the RPA. Fairfax allows redevelopment in an RPA when there is no increase in impervious area and no further encroachment, subject to a Water Quality Impact Assessment. That makes a same-location rebuild the realistic program. What does not pencil is scraping it for the 7,000 square foot, $1.85M to $2.35M spec home the block comps are built on. Those sit on unconstrained lots. This one does not.

So the deal is real, just materially constrained and approval-dependent. It is a flood-safe Pimmit Hills lot for a same-envelope custom rebuild or a renovation, not a teardown-to-mansion play.
