Block Three · about fifteen minutes

Write it, or you do not have it.

Reading is recognition. Writing is retrieval, and retrieval is the part that sticks. Everything here saves as you type and starts clean tomorrow.

Morning pages
Three minutes, whatever is at the top of your mind. No topic, no edit, no audience. Yapping is clarity of thought in sixty seconds, and clarity of thought comes from writing. This is the block that makes the other blocks work, so it goes first.
The negotiation rep
Take one tactic off today's cards and write it at a live deal. Not the theory, the actual sentence you would say out loud. If you cannot write it, you do not have it yet.
The content gym
The four-part ritual, written every day whether or not anything gets shot. Hook is an open loop, not a thesis they can grab and leave.
The ammo line
One backable number or one reference from outside real estate, aimed at today's angle. You do not write the piece from your opinion, you write it from the ammo. If the number is not in the facts bank and you cannot source it, it does not go on screen.
The spoken yap
Sixty seconds out loud, on the phone, one take. The bar is that you recorded it, not that it was good. Ums and pauses and a restart are the format working, not the format failing. The second you try to optimize a yap you are doing the one thing a yap is not for. Post it or bin it, either counts.
Where it goes today
Two lines. One conversation the negotiation card is aimed at, one thing that gets shot. This is the part that turns study into a habit instead of a hobby.
