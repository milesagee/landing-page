The Assignment

The Hill That Named Richmond

6 to 7 minutes, CINEMATIC mode, Contrast shape. New footage needed: none. every frame already exists on the drive. Every clip is named below, with a copy button for the path.

The hook, say it exactly

Everybody who tells you Richmond is boring has never stood on this hill at eight o'clock.

Open or closed: Open. It stakes a claim the viewer has to test and withholds both the hill and what happens on it. Topic is clear in two seconds.

The feeling to protect

Effortless belonging. The feeling that this is a normal weeknight for people who live here and you are the only one who did not know about it. Not spectacle, not an event ad, not FOMO. The moment it starts performing how special it is, the viewer stops believing it. Cut it like you are already a regular and you are just showing a friend where to park.

The trap

The party cut. Crowd, drone push, sun down, music, done. It will look gorgeous, it will get a nice comment section, and it recruits exactly the wrong buyer. The same footage with the cargo attached does both jobs at once. If a chapter has no cargo, it is B-roll, not a chapter.

The one thing the whole video has to carry

Libby Hill is the overlook Richmond was named for, it sits in Church Hill, and standing on that side of the view has a price attached that has moved more than most of the city. The video is a place-and-price argument wearing a sunset.

Hard Gate

The cargo is price, not people.

The cargo is price, inventory, and days on market. Never who lives there and never who is arriving.

WhyThe event note in the footage library describes the crowd as a live picture of who is moving into the East End. That is a fine internal observation and it is an unusable on-camera line. Church Hill is a historically Black neighborhood in fast price change, and characterizing who lives in or moves to a neighborhood is the exact pattern the audience gate drops on sight. It is also the kind of line that ends a license.
Never say
  • Any description of who lives in Church Hill or the East End, or who is moving there.
  • Any 'the neighborhood is changing' framing that means people rather than prices.
  • Safe, sketchy, up-and-coming, transitional, good schools, family-friendly. All of them.
  • Any shot held long enough to make an individual in the crowd the subject.
Say instead
  • Median price, price per square foot, days on market, active inventory, permits, what is under construction.
  • The history of the overlook and the naming of the city.
  • Wide crowd texture where no one person is the subject.
Bea and Cess have never been to Richmond and cannot be expected to feel where this line sits. That is why it is a gate on the brief and not a footnote.
