Before You Count On Anything

Do your own homework.

Virginia is a buyer-beware state. Whatever you picture for this lot, confirm it with the county before you count on it. Here is where a serious buyer starts.

  • Resource Protection Area (RPA)
    A 1993 Chesapeake Bay Resource Protection Area overlays most of this parcel. Redevelopment in an RPA is governed by Fairfax County ordinance and can require a WQIA and a site-specific delineation. Confirm the boundary and your options with a licensed surveyor or engineer and Fairfax Land Development Services before you plan any footprint.
  • Floodplain
    Part of the parcel edges FEMA Zone AE. The primary building envelope appears to sit in Zone X, outside the special flood hazard area, but that is not a substitute for an official determination. Pull the FEMA panel and confirm.
  • Buildable envelope and setbacks
    R-4 zoning sets the setbacks, lot coverage, and impervious limits. What can actually be built or rebuilt here, and by what approval path, is a county question. Do not assume the comps up the street define what this specific lot allows.
  • Survey and lot area
    The recorded land area and the county GIS geometry differ slightly. Order a current survey for any transaction decision.
These are the questions a serious buyer asks first. Bring your engineer, your surveyor, and Fairfax County into it early. Reach out and I will point you to the parcel records to start.
